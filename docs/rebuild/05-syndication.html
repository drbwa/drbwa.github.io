The Substack half of the plan: what to add to the build, what to write once, and the per-post checklist that keeps the site canonical rather than the copy.

How this document relates to the others

Docs 02, 03 and 04 are unchanged and still authoritative for everything they cover. This document carries only the additions syndication requires, each one tagged with the file it belongs in and the task it follows. § 1

The one rule

Non-negotiable

The site publishes first. Always. Substack gets a copy afterwards, with a link back in the first paragraph.

Substack does not let a publisher set an external canonical tag — every post there asserts itself as canonical on substack.com. That is not a problem as long as your version exists first and the Substack copy points at it. It becomes a problem the first time you draft straight into Substack because it was quicker, because then the copy that gets cited, linked and indexed is the one you do not own. Over a few years that quietly relocates your archive onto someone else's platform.

This is R-07 in DOC 01. It is the only thing in this document that actually matters; everything else is mechanics.

The division of labour that follows from it:

  • The site owns the canonical URL, the archive, the RSS feed, the diagrams and maths as they were meant to look, and anything a paper or a profile links to.
  • Substack owns the mailing list, the discovery surface, and the comments (DOC 01 Q4).
  • Nothing is owned twice. No comments on the site, no analytics duplicated, no attempt to mirror subscriber counts anywhere.

D-03

Subscribe.astro — one sentence, no iframe

src/components/ · new file
Do not embed Substack's subscribe widget

Substack's embeddable form is an <iframe> pulling third-party JavaScript. Dropping it in would put a tracked external frame on every post and destroy the zero-JavaScript property that DOC 04 § 2.2 tests for. Link to the Substack page instead. One fewer network origin, one fewer thing to break, and the subscribe flow on Substack's own page converts better than an embedded box anyway.

Render it in exactly two places: once at the foot of src/pages/blog/index.astro, and once at the foot of src/layouts/Post.astro after the article content. Not on the home page, not in the header, not in the footer — the footer already carries the Newsletter link from D-02.

D-04

Syndication.astro — the "also published" line

src/components/ · new file

Render it in src/layouts/Post.astro, between the article content and <Subscribe />, passing post.data.syndication. When the array is empty it renders nothing at all, which is the state every post is in on the day it goes live.

D-05

Styles for both

Derived from the existing tokens — no new colours, no box, no button.

§ 3

The per-post checklist

Roughly ten minutes per post once you have done it twice. Extends DOC 04 § 6.6.

Publish on the site

Remove draft: true, build, commit, push. Actions deploys. Do not open Substack yet.

Start the Substack post and paste the prose

New post, paste the body. Substack accepts Markdown as you type, so most of it lands intact. What does not is in § 4.

Put the link back in the first paragraph

Not the footer — the first paragraph, above the fold, where an email reader sees it without scrolling.

Standard opening line

Originally published at brunowassermann.com/blog/<slug>/, where the diagrams and the maths render properly.

Repair what did not survive

Diagrams become images, display maths goes into a LaTeX block, tables get rebuilt. Work through the table in § 4 rather than from memory.

Send

Substack handles the email. Nothing to do on the site.

Record the syndication and push again

Add the Substack URL to the post's front matter. This is what makes the "Also published on Substack" line appear, and it is the only step that is easy to forget.

If you ever want the reverse

Substack can do a one-time import from an RSS feed, which is how you would seed the newsletter if the site ever accumulates a backlog before the Substack exists. It is one-time only — there is no ongoing RSS sync, so do not design a workflow around it. Third-party auto-posting services exist; they add a dependency and an account for something that is a ten-minute manual step a few times a year.

Exporting a Mermaid diagram for Substack

Copy the contents of the mermaid fence into a scratch file and render it. White background, because that is what a Substack email is.

--scale 3 gives a crisp image on a high-density screen. -t neutral matches the site's Mermaid theme from DOC 02 T1.3, so the two versions look like the same diagram. The alternative is to open the live post and save the rendered SVG out of the browser, which is faster once but not reproducible.

§ 5

Setting up the Substack, once

Create the publication

Handle brunowassermann, giving brunowassermann.substack.com.

Do not buy a Substack custom domain

Substack charges a one-time fee to map a domain, and doing it would put a second host on your domain asserting itself as canonical — the exact problem § 1 exists to prevent. The site owns the domain; Substack is the mailing list. A subdomain of substack.com is the correct address for it.

Turn off what you do not want

In Settings, disable the subscribe pop-up and the recommendations modal if you find them intrusive on your own posts, and decide whether to enable Substack's cross-recommendation of other publications. None of this affects the site.

§ 6

Delta to the design and copy

DOC 03 § 1 forbids a "Let's connect!" call to action, and that rule stands. A subscribe line is not that rule's target, so the boundary needs stating explicitly rather than left to the implementer's judgement.

AllowedNot allowed
One sentence of body text with two links in it, at the foot of the writing index and the foot of each post. Set at caption size in the muted ink, above a hairline rule. A bordered or filled box. A button. An email input field. Substack's embed iframe. A pop-up or slide-in of any kind. The word "Join". An exclamation mark. Any of it in the header, the home page, or mid-article.

The subscribe line — final copy

New posts go out by email. Subscribe, or use the RSS feed.

§ 7

Delta to the launch runbook

Four checks to add to DOC 04. The third is the one that matters — it is the regression test for the zero-JavaScript property, now that there is a live external link on every post.

Add toCheck
§ 1.2 pagesThe subscribe line appears at the foot of /blog/ and at the foot of a post, and nowhere else. grep -c 'class="subscribe"' dist/index.html returns 0.
§ 1.3 pipelineA post with an empty syndication array renders no "Also published" line at all — not an empty paragraph, not a stray full stop. A post with one entry renders exactly one link.
§ 2.2 no-JSA post page still references no bundled JavaScript beyond Mermaid where a diagram exists. Specifically: grep -c 'iframe' dist/blog/*/index.html returns 0. If it does not, someone pasted the Substack embed.
§ 3.2 accessBoth links in the subscribe line are reachable by keyboard and have a visible focus ring. The line is body text, so it needs no aria anything.
And one thing to verify by hand, per post, forever

That the Substack copy carries the link back in its first paragraph. Nothing automated will catch this, and it is the single point of failure for the rule in § 1.
